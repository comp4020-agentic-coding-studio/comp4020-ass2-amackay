## The axioms are schemes

set.mm's axioms "should be interpreted as schemes, or recipes, for generating
those axioms and theorems".

[Proof Explorer, note on the axioms](https://us.metamath.org/mpeuni/mmset.html#axiomnote)

```notes
A scheme is a recipe with variables in it; each way of filling the variables
is one axiom. What set.mm proves are schemes too, which is why one theorem
covers infinitely many cases.
```

---

## Where they come from

The system "is founded on a simplified formalization of predicate calculus
with equality published by logician Alfred Tarski in 1965".

[Proof Explorer, note on the axioms](https://us.metamath.org/mpeuni/mmset.html#axiomnote)

```notes
Tarski's system is the base; set.mm adds schemes to it. The next slide says
what the additions buy.
```

---

## Scheme completeness

The extra schemes give "scheme completeness (defined in Remark 9.6 of
[Megill] and called "metalogical completeness" there)".

Norman Megill,
[A Finitely Axiomatized Formalization of Predicate Calculus with Equality](http://projecteuclid.org/euclid.ndjfl/1040149359)
(1995) · [preprint](https://us.metamath.org/downloads/finiteaxiom.pdf)

```notes
The 1995 paper is where the axiom system was worked out. The preprint on the
site is the readable copy; the journal page is the citation.
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

"$d x y simply means that x and y must be distinct, i.e. they may not be
simultaneously substituted with the same variable. The statement $d x ϕ means
variable x must not occur in wff ϕ."

— [The Metamath book](https://us.metamath.org/downloads/metamath.pdf)

```notes
The whole meaning of $d in two sentences. A $d statement is the price of the
simple axiom system: the scheme is only sound when these variables stay
apart.
```

## Not part of the logic

$d conditions "are metalogical conditions imposed on certain axiom and theorem
schemes. They have no role in the actual logic (object language)".

Wednesday's lab removes one and watches the verifier object.

[Proof Explorer, Appendix 3](https://us.metamath.org/mpeuni/mmset.html#distinct)

```notes
Metalogical: a condition on the recipe, not a statement in the language. The
verifier enforces it at substitution time, which is the error you will read
on Wednesday.
```

## What a Metamath system means

Mario Carneiro,
[Models for Metamath](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1601.07699) (2016):

- uses them to show the main database consistent if ZFC has a model

```notes
As the abstract is indexed. This is the semantic side of the course: not
whether a proof checks, but what a checked proof is about.
```

## One parse per formula

Carneiro's
[Grammar ambiguity in set.mm](https://us.metamath.org/downloads/grammar-ambiguity.txt)
(2015) proves set.mm's syntax has one parse per formula, since "without this
constraint, the task of finding syntax proofs can become just as difficult as
finding logical proofs".

```notes
Why the assistants can find syntax proofs for you: the grammar is
unambiguous, and a 2015 note proves it. Your verifier gets to assume it.
```
