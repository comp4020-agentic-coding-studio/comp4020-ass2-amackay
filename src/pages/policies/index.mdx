# Policies and support

## Deadlines and extensions

Lab proofs are due by 5pm on the Friday of their week. The definition exercise
and the verifier project have their own due dates on their pages. Work
submitted after a deadline without an extension is not marked. Extensions are
granted for the asking if you ask before the deadline; email the convenor with
the date you can meet. After the deadline, an extension needs a reason.

## Working together

Talk about proofs with anyone. A proof is checked by a program, and two people
who arrive at the same proof by talking have both learned what the program
needs. What you submit must be typed by you, and the write-ups you submit must
be in your words. The verifier project is the exception: its code is written
by you alone, in whatever language you choose, and you may read the language
specification and the test suite but not another verifier's source.

## Tools

Any editor, any operating system, any proof assistant. The course teaches
mmj2 and the labs assume it, but a proof that verifies is a proof. Language
models and other generators may be used for the lab proofs and the definition
exercise; say so in one line when you submit. They may not be used to write
the verifier project's code.

## What you may do with what you produce

The databases the course works in are public domain: the Metamath project's
[copyright page](https://us.metamath.org/copyright.html) says its databases
"are waived of all rights, including copyright, according to the CC0 Public
Domain Dedication". A proof you write in a lab is yours to publish, contribute
or forget. This site's own text is licensed as the footer says.

## Getting help

The lab is the place to get unstuck. Between labs, email the
[tutor](/people/teodor-ashby/) with the proof file attached, or the
[convenor](/people/noor-castellane/) for anything that is not a proof.
